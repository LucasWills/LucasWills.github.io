Sodium Metal Production

Sodium Metal from Drain Cleaner via Alcohol Catalyzed Magnesium Reduction

2025

WARNING: This project works with highly reactive and flamable sodium metal at high temperatures, as well as extremely caustic sodium hydroxide. This project presents a risk of fire if care is not taken. If you chose to replicate this, you are doing so at your own risk.

Background

It has been known for a few decades that potassium and the heavier alkali metals could be produced by a relatively low temperature reduction catalyzed by tertiary alcohols. Potassium hydroxide and magnesium turnings are added to a suitable solvent, as well as a few mL of tert-butanol or tert-amyl alcohol, and the mixture is heated to a bit under 200°C for a few hours, where large drops of potassium metal will slowly form. However, this method didn't seem to be able to produce sodium metal at all. If it was able to produce sodium, this would be a very attractive method to produce the useful metal for amature chemists as it doesn't involve extremely dangerous molten caustic salts like the Castner process, and didn't involve massive pyrotechnic fireballs like the popular approach of burning a mixture of sodium hydroxide and magnesium powder.

In 2018, NurdRage demonstrated their discovery that this method could actually be successful at producing sodium, it was just much slower and required much more specific conditions. After some optimization, they arrived at a suitable procedure achieving >90% purity producing sodium at a decent scale using amature-available precursors. However, this procedure has proven to still be quite finicky, and many amature chemists have had trouble reproducing it, including myself at first, although I ultimately was able to get it to work quite well after a few attempts.

In this page, I will outline my findings with this method, what I've found gets the best results and what causes problems.

Reagents and Where to Get Them

  1. Magnesium Metal

    Magnesium must be relatively finely divided, but should also not be significantly oxidized. This means that if it is extremely finely divided, like magnesium powder or dust, it should be completely fresh and unopened otherwise it may be too oxidized. The magnesium must also be relatively pure, high quality magnesium. Low quality magnesium turnings will often not work. I tried cheap magnesium turnings from Amazon sold as fire starters but had basically no success. I had the best results with 35 mesh magnesium granules from United Nuclear. Another option is to purchase high purity magnesium ingot or ribbon, and shave or cut small pieces off of it. I haven't tried this, but I believe it would work well.

  2. Sodium Hydroxide

    This reaction is not too picky about NaOH. Crystal lye drain opener works fine. I used Rooto branded '100% Lye', which comes as very small prills. Powder should work fine as well.

  3. Alcohol Catalyst

    The most widely available catalyst is menthol, however it is quite slow. The reaction will take 20-30 hours to complete if using menthol. Other good options are borneol and tetrahydrolinalool. Tetrahydrolinalool will take up to 20 hours, and borneol will take around 10. The reaction may occur somewhat faster if using very fine magnesium and sodium hydroxide, but I have not tested this since fresh magnesium powder of high enough quality may be difficult to get. I chose to use borneol because the faster reaction time meant I didn't have to run it overnight. I got my borneol from Perfumer's Apprentice. Other sources should be fine as long as it's in the form of pure crystals or crystaline powder.

  4. Mineral Oil

    Mineral oil needs to be free of any aromatic compounds or alkenes which will destroy the sodium as it's produced. For this reason, make sure it's marked as odorless and pure. Hypoallergenic baby oil works well. I had an easier time and similar results using this mineral oil, marked as pure odorless food grade mineral oil for cutting boards. I like it because it has a lower viscosity, making it a little less messy.

A Note about Stirring

Strong stirring is absolutely critical for this reaction to work. The entire reaction mixture should be suspended in the mineral oil, with no material resting on the bottom of the flask. I used the largest stirbar that could fit in my flask. If the stirring is even slightly too poor to keep everything in suspension, yield will be terrible.

Procedure:

  1. 14g of Magnesium and 20g of NaOH were added to a 250mL Erlenmeyer flask with a ground glass joint, and a strong 50mm magnetic stir bar inside. On top of that about 100-150mL of mineral oil was added, followed by 3g of sodium or lithium metal to jumpstart the reaction and prevent corrosion of the glass.
  2. About 1.5g of borneol was crushed into a powder and added to the flask.
  3. A greased vacuum adapter was added to the top of the flask and attacked with a metal keck clip.
  4. A transparent rubber hose was attached to the vacuum adapter, and the other end was dipped into a cylinder with a small amount of mineral oil at the bottom. This serves as a bubbler to let hydrogen gas out while preventing entry of oxygen.
  5. A thermocouple was strapped to the outside of the flask roughly in the middle of the height of the liquid, using high temperature tape. alternatively, a thermocouple may be dipped into the liquid using a glass thermowell in a claisen adapter. I did both and found that the external thermocouple was accurate enough, provided that everything is well insulated with aluminum foil.
  6. The flask was then heated to about 120°C and stirring was turned up to at least 800RPM. This should be able to put all of the material into suspension.
  7. The mixture was allowed to stir at this temperature for at least an hour. During this time, some hydrogen bubbles will be visible coming from the bubbler. This is good, it shows that the mineral oil and reagents are being dried by the lithium or sodium jumpstart. It should be kept at this temperature until this bubbling stops or gets very slow, like one bubble every minute. Usually 90 minutes is fine.
  8. Next, the temperature was raised to about 200°C. Bubbles start to come through the bubbler again, and speed up as the temperature increases. It's important to try to avoid the temperature climbing above 210°C, as this will begin to boil off the borneol or menthol catalyst. This is more important with borneol because it does not condense, instead forming crystals on the glass above the reaction mixture, as seen below. This will occur to some degree even at lower temperatures, but it's not a major issue.
    The reaction mixture turns into a murky grey mixture over the course of the reaction, and eventually, tiny droplets of sodium may become visible.
  9. The mixture was kept at around 200°C until bubbling stopped and, the bubbler started to slowly backflow, which usually takes around 10 hours with borneol. If this does not occur, the reaction can be stopped when it's producing something like one bubble every 1-2 minutes without losing much yield. When the reaction is ready to stop, heating is stopped and stirring is gradually lowered to something slow, like 100RPM. While the mixture slowly cools but the sodium is still above its melting point at around 100°C, it will slowly coalesce into larger blobs with the help of the slow stirring. A large sodium blob is shown at the bottom of the flask in the picture below.
  10. Once the temperature lowered to around 120°C, stirring was stopped and everything was allowed to cool to room temperature.
  11. Once cool, the murky liquid was poured off into a waste beaker, leaving behind a large piece of sodium metal.
  12. Cutting the sodium chunk in half reveals that, while there is a lot of sodium, there's also a significant amount of magnesium bits trapped inside it. This is fine for most purposes, including feeding forward into future runs of this reaction, but the best way to purify further is by stirring the sodium in refluxing 1,4-dioxane for a few hours.

    Then we stir vigorously in refluxing dioxane for 2 hours.

    Then, we lower the stirring from 800RPM to 150RPM, let it continue to reflux for 30 minutes, then lower stirring to 60RPM and let it go for an additional 30 minutes.

    The resulting sodium balls were quite pure on the inside, but still had a crust of magnesium on the outside. This could be scraped off very easily, but I chose to repeat the dioxane process one more time.
